# BulkElectronDensity

The bulk electron density ρₑ of the **buffer**, with its standard uncertainty. The measured macromolecule itself is never a solute here. The module owns the buffer solute types and has one exit point.

## Public API

- `Solute` (abstract) with the concrete `Protein`, `NonBiological`, `DNA` and `RNA`, each carrying `molarity`, `molarity_uncertainty` and an `arg` (sequence or name); defined in `BulkElectronDensity.jl`. Protein, DNA and RNA solutes are buffer components (for example a carrier protein), not the measured species.
- `DEFAULT_TEMPERATURE_C`: the default sample temperature (25 °C).
- `ρₑ(pH, σ_pH, solutes; t = DEFAULT_TEMPERATURE_C) -> (ρₑ, σρₑ)`: the bulk electron density in e·Å⁻³ and its first-order propagated uncertainty. The formula is in its docstring. `Inference.ρₑ_prior` wraps this result in a LogNormal and nothing else of the module is used outside it.

Everything below (the partial molar volume ϕ° backends, their tables and the water density `_ρₑ_w`) is internal: the data and provenance sections document where the numbers come from.

## Internals: partial molar volumes

Partial molar volume (V0, cm3/mol) data for solutes and solvent, per solute class, combined with the Kell water density to give ρₑ. Each class keeps its data and its code together:

| Folder | Code | Data |
|---|---|---|
| `NonBiological/` | `NonBiological.jl`: `ϕ°(name)`, the alias resolution, `_solutes`, `NONBIO_LOOKUP` | `nonbiological.{tsv,json}`, `nonbio_lookup.json` |
| `Nucleotide/` | `Nucleotide.jl`: `ϕ°` of a DNA or RNA sequence, the per-nucleotide and ionization tables, the ambiguity codes | `DNA/` and `RNA/`, each with `dna`/`rna.{tsv,json}` and `ionization.{tsv,json}` |
| `Protein/` | `Protein.jl`: `ϕ°(pH, seq)`, the side-chain and ionization tables, the ambiguity codes | `protein.{tsv,json}`, `ionization.{tsv,json}` |
| `Polymers/` | `Polymers.jl`: the group-contribution families and the fixed grades | `polymers.{tsv,json}`, `polymers_lookup.json` |

`BulkElectronDensity.jl` holds the solute types, the constants and the water density, and includes these in dependency order; `Titration.jl` holds the helpers that `Protein.jl` and `Nucleotide.jl` share (the pH-dependent volume of an ionizable residue and the average over an ambiguity code); the generic function `ϕ°` is declared in `BulkElectronDensity.jl`; `CalcDensity.jl` combines everything into ρₑ.

## General assumptions

- At typical buffer  concentrations (~10-150 mM) a few cm3/mol of V0 error moves the electron density by a fraction of a percent. Exact protonation state, temperature correction, etc. are only worth  chasing for solutes pushed to molar concentration (ex: 6 M urea, 50% w/w glycerol), where V0 error propagates enough to bias the fit. Users must input custum arguments to make it work.
- **Additivity / dilute-limit assumption.** Solution volume is taken as V(solution) ≈ V0(water) + Σ nᵢ·V0(soluteᵢ) at infinite dilution. Young's rule / ideal-mixing hold that the rules for low concentrations, and assume zero interactions between solutes.
- **Dissociation convention.** Strong electrolytes are stored and entered as dissociated ions (a salt's V0 is the sum of its ion V0s, on the conventional scale with V(H+) = 0, see "Ion volume scale"); weak acids and bases are stored as their individual protonation forms, whose molarities follow from the pH; ion pairs and complexes are not stored (for example OBIGT's neutral NaCl, KCl or CaSO4 are ion pairs and sit about 11.7 cm3/mol above the dissociated salts, so they must not be mixed in). **Known limitation:** ion pairing at higher concentration. At 10 mM Mg2+ with ATP, EDTA or phosphate, or Ca2+ with sulfate, a good fraction forms complexes whose volume differs from the free-ion sum. The effect on the density is small at buffer concentrations but not zero. Treating it properly needs a speciation step upstream of this table; it is a separate problem and no pair rows are stored.
- **Units**: V0 in cm3/mol, at 298.15 K and ~0.1 MPa unless a source states otherwise. `uncertainty_cm3_per_mol` is the source's own reported standard error, not a subjective error bar; left blank where a source doesn't report one. At lookup (`ϕ°(name)`), a blank uncertainty is replaced by the **mean of every stated uncertainty in nonbiological.json**, so adding rows can shift it slightly (and with it the fits). Away from 25 °C, `BulkElectronDensity.ρₑ` widens each ϕ° uncertainty by `PMV_FRACTIONAL_EXPANSIBILITY · ϕ° · |t − 25|` (3.0 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹; derived from the multi-temperature sugar/urea/glycoluril/nucleobase series in these tables, and exceeded by some electrolytes, e.g. NaH2PO4 ≈ 5.3 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹).

## NonBiological/

pH-dependent protonation has to be applied at read time.

### nonbiological.tsv

Columns: `common_name`, `iupac_name`, formula, charge, `v0_cm3_per_mol`, `uncertainty_cm3_per_mol`, basis, doi. IUPAC names have not been verified yet. Ionic V0 values are on the conventional scale (V(H⁺) = 0), see "Ion volume scale" below; `ion_scale_shift.tsv` beside it records the rows converted from the absolute scale.

Caveats:

- Many rows cite Krumgalz/Pogorelsky/Pitzer 10.1063/1.555981 or Millero 10.1021/cr60270a001 which are compilations. Where they disagree, one was picked arbitrarily.
- KH2PO4/K2HPO4/K3PO4/Na3PO4/NaH2PO4/LiH2PO4/Na2HPO4 (DOI 10.1016/j.jct.2013.01.012) were back-calculated from raw density data via Masson-equation extrapolation (Vφ = V0 + Sv·√m), since the source only reports density/sound velocity. None of them is selected: the sodium salts are superseded by Lo Surdo 1979 measurements, and KH2PO4/K2HPO4 are `rejected` (see "Data conventions" below). K3PO4 25.2 and LiH2PO4 34.9 are `rejected` as well (2:1 and 3:1 salt Masson extrapolations are invalid); K3PO4 is the Lo Surdo 1979 value -3.34.
- trisodium citrate has two independent, disagreeing sources: 57.1 (10.1016/j.molliq.2004.07.014) vs. Apelblat & Manzurola's 69.32 (10.1016/0378-3812(90)85049-G). Cross-checks (agreement with an independent disodium hydrogen citrate measurement, and Apelblat's dissociation-step volume changes) favour 69.32.
- **N-methylimidazole (1-methylimidazole)**: no paper reports a partial molar volume directly; Hou et al. 2018 (*J. Mol. Liquids* **257**, 100–111, DOI 10.1016/j.molliq.2018.02.098) measured density/sound-velocity/etc. across the full N-mim + water composition range and fitted a Redlich-Kister excess-volume polynomial (their Eq. 7/Table 7), but never evaluated it at infinite dilution. basis=derived: V0 = V*(pure N-mim, from their Table 1 density) + `ΔV_N`-mim(x→0) = ΣAᵢ (their Eq. 8 reduces to the coefficient sum at x=0) = 79.66 + (−1.238) ≈ 78.4 cm³/mol at 298.15 K.
- **Unstable solutes with no measurable or estimable aqueous V0**

  - **PMSF**: no aqueous partial molar volume has ever been published, and PMSF hydrolyzes in water with a half-life of 35–110 min (pH-dependent, faster at higher pH), which is incompatible with the slow densitometry these measurements require. It is why PMSF stocks are prepared in anhydrous ethanol/isopropanol rather than water. Cabani's and Gianni's group schemes have zero coverage of the sulfonyl fluoride (-SO2F) group, so no classical group-additivity value is possible. The table row for `phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride` (127.2 ± 5.0, basis=predicted) is therefore **our own geometric estimate for the intact molecule** (see "Adding a missing solute" below), added so the SAXS fixtures can include the 1 mM PMSF in SASDYW6; it is not a measurement and should never be treated as one.
  - **Benzamidine, AEBSF**: no aqueous volumetric literature found; no attempt made to estimate either.
  - **Practically**: V0 error only matters for solutes pushed toward molar concentration. PMSF is used at 0.1–1 mM specifically because it's unstable/toxic at higher concentration, so an exact V0 for it would not meaningfully affect the fit even if one existed.
- **Gianni & Lepori 1996** ("Group Contributions to the Partial Molar Volume of Ionic Organic Solutes in Aqueous Solution," *J. Solution Chem.* **25**, 1–22, DOI 10.1007/BF00972756) has a large appendix of ~300 charged-solute values. Of these, **95 monofunctional ions** were reconstructed from Gianni's abbreviated notation (e.g. MeNH3+ → methylammonium) into real names, resolved via PubChem, and merged. The table is on the **absolute** (TATB) ion scale; every one of these rows that sat on the live JSON (73 in all, with a few more added later) has been shifted to the conventional scale (see "Ion volume scale" below).
- **2-Mercaptoethanol (BME) and its close analogs**  No aqueous partial molar volume has ever been experimentally measured for any simple aliphatic thiol (confirmed independently by Cabani 1981, Schulte & Rogers 2004 10.1016/j.gca.2003.06.001, and Plyasunova/Plyasunov/Shock 2005 10.1021/je0497045, the last of these states outright that "data leading to the evaluation of the partial molar volumes of S-containing organic compounds are virtually nonexistent"). Every aliphatic-thiol V0 value in this file, including the whole methanethiol–dodecanethiol series, traces back to **one single unverified assumption**: Schulte & Rogers' substitution of the cysteine−serine partial-molar-volume difference (12.87 cm³/mol, from Millero, Lo Surdo & Shin 1978) as a stand-in for the missing aliphatic -SH vs. -OH group contribution, since Cabani's own group-contribution scheme only tabulates an aromatic SH(φ) value (thiophenol-type, 15.13 cm³/mol) and not an aliphatic one.

  - 2-Mercaptoethanol (69.0 ± 3.5), Cysteamine (72.2 ± 3.5), 1,2-Ethanedithiol (81.9 ± 5.0), and 2,2'-Thiodiethanol (101.4 ± 5.0) are *our own* estimates built from Cabani's Az/CH2/OH/NH2/S group values plus the derived aliphatic-SH increment (≈19.6 cm³/mol = Cabani's OH + the 12.87 cysteine-serine transfer) and a proxy OH–Y interaction correction (−2.3 cm³/mol, averaged from Cabani's other known n=2 bifunctional pairs, since no OH–SH pair correction exists). Cross-checked two independent ways (building up from Az+groups vs. adjusting Schulte's own ethanethiol estimate) that agreed to within 0.2 cm³/mol. 1,2-Ethanedithiol's uncertainty is wider because it uses the unverified SH estimate *twice*.
  - Methanethiol through 1-Dodecanethiol (except propanethiol) are Schulte & Rogers' values.
  - 1-Propanethiol uses Plyasunova/Plyasunov/Shock's refined "accepted" value (83.6 ± 1.0) instead of Schulte's original (83.57, no stated uncertainty) since it's the same underlying estimate with a properly quantified uncertainty.

### nonbiological.json

`iupac_name` -> [`electron_count`, `v0_cm3_per_mol`, `uncertainty_cm3_per_mol`]. `electron_count` (sum of atomic numbers − charge). Where multiple source rows share one `iupac_name`, basis=measured is taken over derived/predicted; ties are broken by lower `uncertainty_cm3_per_mol`; finally if nothing breaks tie they are picked arbitrarily.

**Selection rule** (the JSON matches it for every species; every value equals one TSV row): rank rows by `basis` (measured < derived < predicted < any other value), drop `rejected` rows, then take the lowest stated uncertainty (a blank uncertainty ranks *last*), then ties are arbitrary (a JSON value must always equal one of the tied rows).

**Extra `basis` values** (they rank below `predicted`, so they only win when a species has nothing else):

- `rejected`: row is known to be wrong and never selected. Reasons are in the data conventions below.
- `other-T`: valid measurement but at 18/40/55 °C of an 18/25/40/55 °C series (Chalikian et al., 10.1016/S0301-4622(01)00200-9); the 25 °C row of the series keeps `measured`.
- `cited-unverified`: a value a paper quotes from another source that was not read (Polymers: Ficoll 70 and one dextran row of Acosta 2017). It ranks below everything measured, derived or predicted.
- `unverified-T`: series (10.1016/j.molliq.2020.115210, 10.1007/s10953-008-9302-2, 10.1016/j.jct.2014.10.015, 10.1016/j.jct.2017.04.005) whose temperature/medium is not recorded in the TSV and whose values disagree with 25 °C water sources (e.g. adenine ~80-86 vs ~90). Recover the temperature/medium from the papers before using.

**Data conventions.** Rows are 25 °C, aqueous, anhydrous basis (hydration water and a counter-ion such as HCl are treated as bulk solvent). Rows at other temperatures carry `basis=other-T` and a `[T=… K]` suffix in the doi column. `derived` rows were computed here from a paper's own density or `V_phi` data (Masson √m or linear fit; uncertainty the fit SE, or the larger of SE and the √m-vs-linear intercept difference), and printed ± values are copied as printed (some papers print 95 % confidence half-widths, ≈3.2 × SE). A compilation whose rows are shifted or contradicted by an independent source and a chemical-consistency check is marked `rejected` per row (for example the off-by-one segment of 10.1007/978-3-642-71114-`5_2`, five rows of Millero 1971 (10.1021/cr60270a001) that do not match independent sources (perchlorate 40.7 and sulfate 11.1 equal Marcus's 0 °C values, 10.1021/jp9027244, though labelled 25 °C; chlorate 44.12 equals Marcus's 25 °C perchlorate; nitrate 26.2 and carbonate -9.8 sit 3 to 5 cm³/mol from Krumgalz and Marcus; the Millero paper itself was not read, so the cause is inferred from these numeric coincidences, and no selected value changed; the same source's chromate 13.6, permanganate 38.5, nickel -24.0 and zinc -21.6 are `rejected` too and replaced (chromate 19.85 from Marcus 10.1021/jp412330u and Shock 1997, permanganate 42.6 from KMnO4 51.6 of Spitzer 1979 minus K+); nitrite 29.0 is still selected and suspect (Shock 25.0, Gunther 23.2)), and the potassium phosphates of 10.1016/j.jct.2013.01.012, whose sodium salts sit 4-22 cm³/mol above the Lo Surdo 1979 measurements). The potassium phosphates are then derived from the measured sodium salts plus the Na→K swap V(KCl) − V(NaCl) = 10.20 per cation (KH2PO4 40.41 ± 0.5, K2HPO4 23.53 ± 1.0). Values from papers that fail their own consistency checks (density columns that do not reproduce `V_phi`, implausible extrapolations, non-aqueous or elevated-pressure data) are kept in `sources/extraction/`, not merged. The full per-row history is in git.

**Ion volume scale (conventional, V(H⁺) = 0).** Every ionic V0 in these tables is on the **conventional** scale, where V(H⁺, aq) = 0 and each ion's value follows from additivity of measured salt volumes (Millero; Helgeson/Shock 1997; Marcus). The absolute scale (Marcus 2009, eq. 1) differs by V_abs(i) = V_conv(i) + z·V_abs(H⁺), z algebraic, with V_abs(H⁺) = −5.41 cm³/mol at 25 °C (Millero's −5.1 − 0.008t − 1.7×10⁻⁴t² gives −5.406; Marcus 2014 uses −5.4 ± 0.2). Salt volumes are scale-free, so only single-ion rows are affected, and an inconsistent mixture would make cations too small and anions too large by 5.41 per charge in every salt built from them. A source is absolute if it says so (TATB, Ph₄As⁺/BPh₄⁻ assumption, "single-ion", V(H⁺) ≈ −5.4), or if its H⁺ is −5.4/−5.5, or if its ion rows fail an additivity test against measured salts by almost exactly 5.41·z (Na⁺ −1.21 conventional vs −6.62 absolute; Cl⁻ 17.83 vs 23.24). To check a new ion row: `V(salt) − Σνᵢ V(ionᵢ)` should be within about ±1; a residual of about ±5.4 per charge flags the wrong scale. Never add an absolute-scale ion without applying V_conv = V_abs + 5.41·z.

**Shift record.** 77 rows were converted from the absolute to the conventional scale with V_conv = V_abs + 5.41·z (cations up, anions down; uncertainty column unchanged, since the shift's own ±0.2·|z| is below the stated errors; a blank stays blank): the 73 Gianni and Lepori rows (10.1007/BF00972756; 33 cations +1, 4 cations +2, 31 anions −1, 5 anions −2) and, from Marcus and Hefter 2004 (10.1021/cr030047d), hydron (−5.5 → exactly 0, by definition), tetrahexylammonium (395.9 → 401.31), tetraheptylammonium (458.6 → 464.01) and triflate (80.2 → 74.79). The TSV `v0_cm3_per_mol` and the JSON carry the new value; the original absolute values, the evidence and the confidence of every shifted row are in `NonBiological/ion_scale_shift.tsv` (columns name, iupac_name, charge, doi, v0_old_absolute, v0_new_conventional, shift, scale_evidence, confidence). That file is a record only: nothing reads it, and the main TSV doi column is unchanged. No selection changed: the basis ranking is untouched, no shifted species has tied winners, and the three species with several rows (formate, acetate, guanidinium) keep the same winner (the Gianni and Lepori row, `measured`). After the shift formate and acetate agree with the independent derived rows (26.34 vs 26.27; 40.5 vs 40.36/40.46). The median |residual| of the 63 salt-with-ion additivity checks that involve a shifted ion falls from 6.26 to 0.89 (58 improve; the five that worsen are calcium diacetate 72.32, tetrabutylazanium bromide/iodide 295.8/298.65 and sodium benzoate, whose salt or ion value is itself doubtful).

**Not shifted, unverified.** spermidine(3+) (137.5) and spermine(4+) (191.4), 10.1002/jcc.23608, are **probably absolute**: they equal neutral V + z·5.4 exactly (153.7 − 137.5 = 16.2 = 3 × 5.4; 213.0 − 191.4 = 21.6 = 4 × 5.4). The paper was not read and the rows are ±15/20 predictions, so they are left unchanged (recorded with shift 0 in `ion_scale_shift.tsv`). If confirmed, the conventional values are 153.73 and 213.04 (a physically better prediction is the neutral volume minus about 4-5 of electrostriction per charge).

**Open doubts on the ion scale.**

- The Gianni and Lepori scale is inferred from data (19 of 19 salt-testable rows behave as absolute, none as conventional); the paper's text was not read. The Marcus and Hefter 2004 attribution of 10.1021/cr030047d comes from a web search, not a local PDF.
- 56 shifted rows (alkylammoniums, sulfonates, cholates, pyridiniums...) have no salt in these tables to test them and inherit the scale from their source: medium-high. A measured salt of the ion (e.g. sodium methanesulfonate, pyridinium chloride, sodium cholate) or the appendix footnote would settle them.
- V_abs(H⁺) is uncertain by about ±0.2 per charge (Marcus's alternative fit gives −4.75), so the shift is exact only to that.
- Millero nitrite (29.0) and chlorate (44.12) sit 1.0 and 7.35 above the salts in the anion direction; Millero's table is conventional by definition, so this may be an absolute value that slipped in, but the competing salts are weak. Low confidence, unresolved (needs NaNO₂/KNO₂ at 25 °C).
- Not scale problems (left alone): oxalate (30.46 after the shift vs about 24.4 from salts; proposed for rejection), benzoate (87.11 vs sodium benzoate 89.5-90.4), guanidinium (50.01; its salts scatter 46.5-55 with the counter-ion and diguanidinium sulfate 18.66 is an outlier), and phosphate (−25.6 vs −30.4, 4.7 apart, not 16.2).

**Known unresolved disagreements** (left as-is on purpose): compiled ion volumes from different conventions (Krumgalz vs Millero: chlorate, nitrite, cadmium(2+), nickel(2+), cobalt(2+), zinc(2+), perchlorate, nitrate, sulfate, carbonate, thiocyanate); tetrabutylammonium iodide 312.4 vs 298.65; calcium diacetate 72.32 vs 56.74/62.79; guanosine 178.2 (measured) vs 171.68 (derived); tetraalkylammonium bromides (the Gianni and Lepori vs Millero tetraalkylammonium split of 4-6 disappeared with the scale shift, and the formate/acetate ones too). Millero's nitrate, perchlorate, thiocyanate, carbonate, sulfate, cadmium and Co/Ni/Zn/Pb offsets are not multiples of 5.41·z and run in both directions, so they are old-data disagreement, not scale. A consistent single-source policy for ions matters more than any single pick.

### `nonbio_lookup.json`

Lowercase alias -> official preferred IUPAC name (P-IUPAC, not the systematic name) of nonbiological.json (common names, abbreviations such as `pmsf`, formulas, older or trade names, and a self-mapping for each IUPAC name). The target is the **non-stereospecific** preferred IUPAC name (no `R`/`S`, `E`/`Z`, `D`/`L`, `cis`/`trans` or `α`/`β` descriptors, so enantiomers and geometric isomers share one target, except in the rare case where the isomers have genuinely different volumes (more than 1 cm³/mol apart, grade A/B evidence), which are kept as separate targets with the distinguishing descriptor; locants and indicated hydrogen such as `1H` keep their IUPAC spelling); the `iupac_name` column of `nonbiological.tsv` carries that spelling and the keys of `nonbiological.json` are its lowercase form, since `ϕ°` lowercases every name before resolving. Every alias met anywhere (papers, fitting scripts, user input) must be added here; never add an alias as its own JSON key. `ϕ°(name)` resolves through it first, then falls back to the name as given. Both JSON tables are registered with `include_dependency` in `NonBiological.jl`, so editing them invalidates the precompile cache.

## Protein

### Protein.tsv

Columns: Group, Name, `Temperature_C`, Uncertainty, V0, basis, doi. doi/basis were added as per-row columns (previously one fixed source, 10.1039/9781782627043-00542, for the whole file) to accommodate Sec/Pyl below, which are derived rather than measured.

### ionization.tsv

Columns: Group, Name, Reaction, `Temperature_C`, pKa, `pKa_Uncertainty`, dV (cm³/mol), `dV_Uncertainty`, dKS (adiabatic compressibility change, ×10⁻⁴ cm³ mol⁻¹ bar⁻¹), `dKS_Uncertainty`, basis, doi.

Source for all rows except Sec: Lee, Tikhomirova, Shalvardjian & Chalikian, *Biophys. Chem.*, 2008, 134, 185–199 (DOI 10.1016/j.bpc.2008.02.009). doi/basis were added as per-row columns (rather than one fixed source for the whole file) specifically to accommodate Sec, which comes from elsewhere.

**R-basic's `dV_Uncertainty` in Protein.json** (0.1) is a manually-inserted standard placeholder, not a value reported by Lee et al. since they left it blank.

**Sec (selenocysteine)**, row basis=derived (mixed-provenance row), doi = Huber & Criddle:

- pKa = 5.24, a real titration measurement (Huber & Criddle, *Arch. Biochem. Biophys.*, 1967, 122, 164–173, DOI 10.1016/0003-9861(67)90136-1). A frequently-cited companion number, pKa=5.47 (Byun & Kang, *Biopolymers*, 2011, 95, 345–353, DOI 10.1002/bip.21581), is a **computational** DFT/implicit-solvation prediction, not a measurement.
- dV = 4 ± 4 cm³/mol is **not from either of those papers**  and is our own estimate: buffer-ionization ΔV° values for large, "soft" ionizing groups (MOPS/HEPES/Tris-type, sulfonate/amine) run ~5-7 cm³/mol, versus ~12 cm³/mol for Asp/Glu's compact, charge-dense carboxylate. Se⁻ is larger and more polarizable than any of those, so the central estimate sits below that 5-7 range; the ±4 uncertainty is wide enough to admit the true value could be near zero, since the sign itself isn't fully certain for an anion this soft.

**basic** groups (His, Lys, Arg side chains; the -NH2 N-terminus) are neutral at high pH and gain volume on protonation at low pH:

```
V(pH) = V0 + dV / (1 + 10^(pH - pKa))
```

**acidic** groups (Asp, Glu side chains; the -COOH C-terminus) are  neutral (protonated) at low pH and lose volume on deprotonation at high pH:

```LaTeX
V(pH) = V0 - dV / (1 + 10^(pKa - pH))
```

### Adding a missing solute (worked examples: maltoheptaose, udenafil, PMSF)

The SAXS fixtures (`test/fitting_tests/`) flagged three buffer/ligand components as `MISSING FROM PMV LOOKUP`; `ϕ°` throws `ArgumentError("unknown solute")` for a name that resolves to nothing, so a missing solute cannot simply be listed. All three are now in the tables. To add a solute yourself:

1. **Find a value.** Prefer a measured aqueous V0 at 25 °C (anhydrous basis; subtract `n·18.07` for an `n`-hydrate). *Maltoheptaose*: 694.8 cm³/mol from Hourston (1967, PhD thesis, Table 6.1; partial specific volume 0.600 ± 0.005 mL/g × 1153.0 g/mol → ±5.8), basis=measured. If nothing is measured, estimate (step 1b).
   1b. **Estimate, and validate the estimate on molecules whose answer you know.** Our recipe (used for udenafil and PMSF): `V0 = 1.466·V_vdW − 2.881·(NumHDonors + NumHAcceptors) + 9.352` [cm³/mol], with `V_vdW` = RDKit `AllChem.ComputeMolVolume` (ETKDG + MMFF geometry, Å³) × 0.60221 and the polar-site count from `rdkit.Chem.Lipinski`, fitted to 50 measured neutral (non-zwitterionic) solutes in this table (alcohols, amides, ureas, N-heterocycles, sugars, nucleosides, nucleobases). Leave-one-out RMSE 3.3 cm³/mol (median 2.1 %). The training set tops out at ~210 cm³/mol, so extrapolation was checked directly on the measured maltodextrins: error −0.6 % (DP2), −2.0 % (DP3), −1.9 % (DP4), −2.4 % (DP5), −2.6 % (DP6), −2.9 % (DP7, 674 vs 695 cm³/mol), i.e. it underestimates slightly at large size. We therefore quote **±4 %** and basis=predicted: udenafil (C25H36N6O4S, est. 399.6 → **399.6 ± 16.0**), PMSF (C7H7FO2S, est. 127.2 → **127.2 ± 5.0**). Sanity check: 0.77 and 0.73 mL/g, inside the usual 0.70–0.80 mL/g range. Never quote an uncertainty tighter than the validation error of the method that produced the number.
2. **Pick names.** `iupac_name` (lowercased for the JSON key), a `common_name`, and any aliases people actually write (`pmsf`, `phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride`, ...). Check none collides with an existing key first.
3. **Add the TSV row** to `nonbiological.tsv`: `common_name`, `iupac_name`, formula, charge, v0, uncertainty, basis, doi (a DOI, or a citation/method string when there is none).
4. **Add the JSON entry** to `nonbiological.json`: `"<lowercased iupac name>": [electron_count, v0, uncertainty]`, where `electron_count` = Σ atomic numbers − charge (udenafil 276, PMSF 90). The key **must be entirely lowercase** because `NonBiological.jl` lowercases every lookup; a mixed-case key can never be found.
5. **Add aliases** to `nonbio_lookup.json` (lowercase alias → lowercase iupac key that exists in the JSON). Include a self-mapping for a long IUPAC name.
6. **Check.** Every JSON value must equal one of that species' non-`rejected` TSV rows chosen by the selection rule above, all JSON keys lowercase, every alias target present in the JSON. Then add the solute to the fixture's `SOLUTES` with a realistic concentration σ, and remove its `MISSING FROM PMV LOOKUP` note.

Changing a fixture's `SOLUTES` changes its ρₑ prior, so that fixture's stored `res*.txt`/`.png` outputs are stale until it is re-run.

## Nucleotide/ (RNA and DNA)

V0(residue) = V0(free nucleoside) + V0(phosphate group increment). The phosphate increment (17.7 cm³/mol) is estimated from the *only* base with both a measured free-nucleoside and free-nucleotide value in one internally-consistent source (Kishore & Ahluwalia 1990, 10.1007/BF00650644): AMP·Na (189.1) − adenosine (171.4) = 17.7 cm³/mol. (Kishore 1990's own adenosine value is itself a secondhand reproduction of Kishore, Bhat & Ahluwalia, *Biophys. Chem.* 33 (1989) 227–236. The AMP/adenosine pair is the basis for the whole per-letter table  extrapolation  for the phosphate-group increments,  applied uniformly to G/C/U/T's nucleoside values under a base-independenceassumption.

- ionization.tsv (duplicated in both `Nucleotide/RNA/` and `Nucleotide/DNA/` per the phosphate chemistry being shared) has pKa values for adenine/adenosine/guanosine ring protonation-deprotonation equilibria from Christensen, Rytting & Izatt  1970 (10.1021/bi00827a012 .
- ionization.json is populated using the same single physical anchor.

- **pKa is per-base and real**.
- adenine/adenosine/guanosine ring  ionizations are not covered. (pKa exists, from Christensen/Alberty/Lowe, but no ΔV for any of them.

## Polymers/

`Polymer` is its own solute type, next to `NonBiological`, `Protein`, `DNA` and `RNA`: polymers, polydisperse surfactants and short oligomers (peptides, cyclodextrins, oligoglycols), whose ϕ° and electron count scale with the chain. `Polymer(molarity, σ, arg)` takes a grade name or a chain description; the volume is computed from the chain by group contributions where a family exists (`Polymers/Polymers.jl`) and read from `polymers.json` through `polymers_lookup.json` (alias → target, same block format as `nonbio_lookup.json`) otherwise. `NonBiological("PEG-3350")` is an error. Molarity is per mole of chains (g/L divided by the mean molar mass), except dextran, amphipol, PVP and PVA, which are per average monomer unit, and Ficoll, which is per 1000 Da (a crosslinked copolymer has no monomer; its electron count, 540 per 1000 Da, is our estimate from the sucrose and glyceryl-ether composition). Values are 25 °C; the usual temperature widening applies. pH is forwarded, so peptides titrate.
