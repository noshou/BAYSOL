PartialMolarVolumes

Partial molar volume (V0, cm3/mol) data for solutes and solvent. It is used to build the prior on the buffer's bulk electron density ρₑ (Fitting.ρₑ_prior, see the Fitting README). The measured macromolecule itself is never a solute here.

General assumptions

  • At typical buffer concentrations (~10-150 mM) a few cm3/mol of V0 error moves the electron density by a fraction of a percent. Exact protonation state, temperature correction, etc. are only worth chasing for solutes pushed to molar concentration (ex: 6 M urea, 50% w/w glycerol), where V0 error propagates enough to bias the fit. Users must input custum arguments to make it work.
  • Additivity / dilute-limit assumption. Solution volume is taken as V(solution) ≈ V0(water) + Σ nᵢ·V0(soluteᵢ) at infinite dilution. Young's rule / ideal-mixing hold that the rules for low concentrations, and assume zero interactions between solutes.
  • Units: V0 in cm3/mol, at 298.15 K and ~0.1 MPa unless a source states otherwise. uncertaintycm3permol is the source's own reported standard error, not a subjective error bar; left blank where a source doesn't report one. At lookup (ϕ°(name)), a blank uncertainty is replaced by the mean of every stated uncertainty in nonbiological.json, so adding rows can shift it slightly (and with it the fits). Away from 25 °C, `Fitting.ρₑwidens each ϕ° uncertainty byPMVFRACTIONALEXPANSIBILITY · ϕ° · |t − 25|` (3.0 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹; derived from the multi-temperature sugar/urea/glycoluril/nucleobase series in these tables, and exceeded by some electrolytes, e.g. NaH2PO4 ≈ 5.3 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹).

NonBiological/

pH-dependent protonation has to be applied at read time.

nonbiological.tsv

Columns: commonname, iupacname, formula, charge, v0cm3permol, uncertaintycm3permol, basis, doi. IUPAC names have not been verified yet.

Caveats:

  • Many rows cite Krumgalz/Pogorelsky/Pitzer 10.1063/1.555981 or Millero 10.1021/cr60270a001 which are compilations. Where they disagree, one was picked arbitrarily.

  • KH2PO4/K2HPO4/K3PO4/Na3PO4/NaH2PO4/LiH2PO4/Na2HPO4 (DOI 10.1016/j.jct.2013.01.012) were back-calculated from raw density data via Masson-equation extrapolation (Vφ = V0 + Sv·√m), since the source only reports density/sound velocity. Since 2026-10 none of them is selected: the sodium salts are superseded by Lo Surdo 1979 measurements, and KH2PO4/K2HPO4 are rejected (see "2026-10 potassium phosphate fix" below). K3PO4 25.2 and LiH2PO4 34.9 are still unchecked.

  • trisodium citrate has two independent, disagreeing sources: 57.1 (10.1016/j.molliq.2004.07.014) vs. Apelblat & Manzurola's 69.32 (10.1016/0378-3812(90)85049-G). Cross-checks (agreement with an independent disodium hydrogen citrate measurement, and Apelblat's dissociation-step volume changes) favour 69.32.

  • N-methylimidazole (1-methylimidazole): no paper reports a partial molar volume directly; Hou et al. 2018 (J. Mol. Liquids 257, 100–111, DOI 10.1016/j.molliq.2018.02.098) measured density/sound-velocity/etc. across the full N-mim + water composition range and fitted a Redlich-Kister excess-volume polynomial (their Eq. 7/Table 7), but never evaluated it at infinite dilution. basis=derived: V0 = V*(pure N-mim, from their Table 1 density) + ΔV_N-mim(x→0) = ΣAᵢ (their Eq. 8 reduces to the coefficient sum at x=0) = 79.66 + (−1.238) ≈ 78.4 cm³/mol at 298.15 K.

  • Unstable solutes with no measurable or estimable aqueous V0

    • PMSF: no aqueous partial molar volume has ever been published, and PMSF hydrolyzes in water with a half-life of 35–110 min (pH-dependent, faster at higher pH), which is incompatible with the slow densitometry these measurements require. It is why PMSF stocks are prepared in anhydrous ethanol/isopropanol rather than water. Cabani's and Gianni's group schemes have zero coverage of the sulfonyl fluoride (-SO2F) group, so no classical group-additivity value is possible. The table row for phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride (127.2 ± 5.0, basis=predicted) is therefore our own geometric estimate for the intact molecule (see "Adding a missing solute" below), added so the SAXS fixtures can include the 1 mM PMSF in SASDYW6; it is not a measurement and should never be treated as one.
    • Benzamidine, AEBSF: no aqueous volumetric literature found; no attempt made to estimate either.
    • Practically: V0 error only matters for solutes pushed toward molar concentration. PMSF is used at 0.1–1 mM specifically because it's unstable/toxic at higher concentration, so an exact V0 for it would not meaningfully affect the fit even if one existed.

  • Gianni & Lepori 1996 ("Group Contributions to the Partial Molar Volume of Ionic Organic Solutes in Aqueous Solution," J. Solution Chem. 25, 1–22, DOI 10.1007/BF00972756) has a large appendix of ~300 charged-solute values. Of these, 95 monofunctional ions were reconstructed from Gianni's abbreviated notation (e.g. MeNH3+ → methylammonium) into real names, resolved via PubChem, and merged.

  • 2-Mercaptoethanol (BME) and its close analogs No aqueous partial molar volume has ever been experimentally measured for any simple aliphatic thiol (confirmed independently by Cabani 1981, Schulte & Rogers 2004 10.1016/j.gca.2003.06.001, and Plyasunova/Plyasunov/Shock 2005 10.1021/je0497045, the last of these states outright that "data leading to the evaluation of the partial molar volumes of S-containing organic compounds are virtually nonexistent"). Every aliphatic-thiol V0 value in this file, including the whole methanethiol–dodecanethiol series, traces back to one single unverified assumption: Schulte & Rogers' substitution of the cysteine−serine partial-molar-volume difference (12.87 cm³/mol, from Millero, Lo Surdo & Shin 1978) as a stand-in for the missing aliphatic -SH vs. -OH group contribution, since Cabani's own group-contribution scheme only tabulates an aromatic SH(φ) value (thiophenol-type, 15.13 cm³/mol) and not an aliphatic one.

    • 2-Mercaptoethanol (69.0 ± 3.5), Cysteamine (72.2 ± 3.5), 1,2-Ethanedithiol (81.9 ± 5.0), and 2,2'-Thiodiethanol (101.4 ± 5.0) are our own estimates built from Cabani's Az/CH2/OH/NH2/S group values plus the derived aliphatic-SH increment (≈19.6 cm³/mol = Cabani's OH + the 12.87 cysteine-serine transfer) and a proxy OH–Y interaction correction (−2.3 cm³/mol, averaged from Cabani's other known n=2 bifunctional pairs, since no OH–SH pair correction exists). Cross-checked two independent ways (building up from Az+groups vs. adjusting Schulte's own ethanethiol estimate) that agreed to within 0.2 cm³/mol. 1,2-Ethanedithiol's uncertainty is wider because it uses the unverified SH estimate twice.
    • Methanethiol through 1-Dodecanethiol (except propanethiol) are Schulte & Rogers' values.
    • 1-Propanethiol uses Plyasunova/Plyasunov/Shock's refined "accepted" value (83.6 ± 1.0) instead of Schulte's original (83.57, no stated uncertainty) since it's the same underlying estimate with a properly quantified uncertainty.

nonbiological.json

iupacname -> [electroncount, v0cm3permol, uncertaintycm3permol]. electroncount (sum of atomic numbers − charge). Where multiple source rows share one iupacname, basis=measured is taken over derived/predicted; ties are broken by lower uncertaintycm3per_mol; finally if nothing breaks tie they are picked arbitrarily.

Selection rule as actually implemented (verified: the JSON matches this rule for every species; every value equals one TSV row): rank rows by basis (measured < derived < predicted < any other value), drop rejected rows, then take the lowest stated uncertainty (a blank uncertainty ranks last), then ties are arbitrary (85 species had such ties before the 2026-09 audit; a JSON value must always equal one of the tied rows).

Extra basis values (added in the audit; they rank below predicted, so they only win when a species has nothing else):

  • rejected: row is known to be wrong and never selected. Reason is recorded in the 2026-09 audit list below.
  • other-T: valid measurement but at 18/40/55 °C of an 18/25/40/55 °C series (Chalikian et al., 10.1016/S0301-4622(01)00200-9); the 25 °C row of the series keeps measured.
  • unverified-T: series (10.1016/j.molliq.2020.115210, 10.1007/s10953-008-9302-2, 10.1016/j.jct.2014.10.015, 10.1016/j.jct.2017.04.005) whose temperature/medium is not recorded in the TSV and whose values disagree with 25 °C water sources (e.g. adenine ~80-86 vs ~90). Recover the temperature/medium from the papers before using.

2026-10 additions from 24 PDFs in sources/ (243 TSV rows, 15 new species, 5 JSON values changed; every number was checked against the rendered page, and where a paper prints densities and Vphi the Vphi was recomputed from the densities). Conventions: rows are 25 °C aqueous, anhydrous basis (hydration water is treated as bulk water; the hydrate-basis columns of lactose·H2O, maltose·H2O and raffinose·5H2O were ignored and the authors' anhydrous-basis values used). basis=other-T rows (the rows with a [T=… K] suffix in the doi column; ; derived marks values we computed) are the same solutes at other temperatures from the same papers. derived rows were computed by us from the paper's own density/Vphi data (Masson sqrt(m) or linear fit; an amine/ammonium extrapolation from 1-9 mol/kg is marked in the row); the uncertainty is the fit SE, or max(SE, |sqrt - linear intercept|) for the co-solutes isolated from ternary-system solvent densities. 2026-10 potassium phosphate fix. The rows 10.1016/j.jct.2013.01.012 for KH2PO4 (43.5 ± 1.8) and K2HPO4 (34.4 ± 0.7) are now rejected: that source's own sodium salts sit +3.8 (NaH2PO4 34.0), +11.1 (Na2HPO4 14.2) and +22 (Na3PO4 −12.2) cm³/mol above the Lo Surdo 1979 measurements (30.21, 3.13, −34.12), so its potassium values are not credible. Ion additivity holds on the measured sodium salts (Na⁺ −1.21 + H2PO4⁻ 31.45 = 30.24 vs 30.21), so the potassium salts are derived from the measured sodium salts plus the Na→K swap V(KCl) − V(NaCl) = 26.81 − 16.61 = 10.20 per cation: KH2PO4 40.41 ± 0.5 (± = spread of the three NaH2PO4 measurements), K2HPO4 23.53 ± 1.0 (Millero 2010's Na2HPO4 tables disagree by ~2). The other low rows (Millero 10.1063/1.555981 KH2PO4 34.36, whose NaH2PO4 partner 24.124 is the known back-calculation, and the [Emim]Br co-solute isolations 33.1 / 21.0) remain in the TSV but no longer win. Still unchecked from the same jct.2013 source: K3PO4 25.2 and LiH2PO4 34.9.

Known unresolved disagreements (left as-is on purpose): compiled ion volumes from different conventions (Krumgalz vs Millero: chlorate, nitrite, cadmium(2+), nickel(2+), cobalt(2+), zinc(2+), perchlorate, nitrate, sulfate, carbonate, thiocyanate); tetrabutylammonium iodide 312.4 vs 298.65; calcium diacetate 72.32 vs 56.74/62.79; guanosine 178.2 (measured) vs 171.68 (derived); tetraalkylammonium bromides. A consistent single-source policy for ions matters more than any single pick.

commontoiupac.json

Lowercase alias -> lowercase iupacname key of nonbiological.json (common names, abbreviations such as pmsf, and a self-mapping for long IUPAC names). ϕ°(name) resolves through it first, then falls back to the name as given. Both JSON tables are `includedependency`s of PMV.jl, so editing them invalidates the precompile cache.

Protein

Protein.tsv

Columns: Group, Name, Temperature_C, Uncertainty, V0, basis, doi. doi/basis were added as per-row columns (previously one fixed source, 10.1039/9781782627043-00542, for the whole file) to accommodate Sec/Pyl below, which are derived rather than measured.

ionization.tsv

Columns: Group, Name, Reaction, TemperatureC, pKa, pKaUncertainty, dV (cm³/mol), dVUncertainty, dKS (adiabatic compressibility change, ×10⁻⁴ cm³ mol⁻¹ bar⁻¹), dKSUncertainty, basis, doi.

Source for all rows except Sec: Lee, Tikhomirova, Shalvardjian & Chalikian, Biophys. Chem., 2008, 134, 185–199 (DOI 10.1016/j.bpc.2008.02.009). doi/basis were added as per-row columns (rather than one fixed source for the whole file) specifically to accommodate Sec, which comes from elsewhere.

R-basic's dV_Uncertainty in Protein.json (0.1) is a manually-inserted standard placeholder, not a value reported by Lee et al. since they left it blank.

Sec (selenocysteine), row basis=derived (mixed-provenance row), doi = Huber & Criddle:

  • pKa = 5.24, a real titration measurement (Huber & Criddle, Arch. Biochem. Biophys., 1967, 122, 164–173, DOI 10.1016/0003-9861(67)90136-1). A frequently-cited companion number, pKa=5.47 (Byun & Kang, Biopolymers, 2011, 95, 345–353, DOI 10.1002/bip.21581), is a computational DFT/implicit-solvation prediction, not a measurement.
  • dV = 4 ± 4 cm³/mol is not from either of those papers and is our own estimate: buffer-ionization ΔV° values for large, "soft" ionizing groups (MOPS/HEPES/Tris-type, sulfonate/amine) run ~5-7 cm³/mol, versus ~12 cm³/mol for Asp/Glu's compact, charge-dense carboxylate. Se⁻ is larger and more polarizable than any of those, so the central estimate sits below that 5-7 range; the ±4 uncertainty is wide enough to admit the true value could be near zero, since the sign itself isn't fully certain for an anion this soft.

basic groups (His, Lys, Arg side chains; the -NH2 N-terminus) are neutral at high pH and gain volume on protonation at low pH:

V(pH) = V0 + dV / (1 + 10^(pH - pKa))

acidic groups (Asp, Glu side chains; the -COOH C-terminus) are neutral (protonated) at low pH and lose volume on deprotonation at high pH:

V(pH) = V0 - dV / (1 + 10^(pKa - pH))

Adding a missing solute (worked examples: maltoheptaose, udenafil, PMSF)

The SAXS fixtures (test/fitting_tests/) flagged three buffer/ligand components as MISSING FROM PMV LOOKUP; ϕ° throws ArgumentError("unknown solute") for a name that resolves to nothing, so a missing solute cannot simply be listed. All three are now in the tables. To add a solute yourself:

  1. Find a value. Prefer a measured aqueous V0 at 25 °C (anhydrous basis; subtract n·18.07 for an n-hydrate). Maltoheptaose: 694.8 cm³/mol from Hourston (1967, PhD thesis, Table 6.1; partial specific volume 0.600 ± 0.005 mL/g × 1153.0 g/mol → ±5.8), basis=measured. If nothing is measured, estimate (step 1b). 1b. Estimate, and validate the estimate on molecules whose answer you know. Our recipe (used for udenafil and PMSF): V0 = 1.466·V_vdW − 2.881·(NumHDonors + NumHAcceptors) + 9.352 [cm³/mol], with V_vdW = RDKit AllChem.ComputeMolVolume (ETKDG + MMFF geometry, Å³) × 0.60221 and the polar-site count from rdkit.Chem.Lipinski, fitted to 50 measured neutral (non-zwitterionic) solutes in this table (alcohols, amides, ureas, N-heterocycles, sugars, nucleosides, nucleobases). Leave-one-out RMSE 3.3 cm³/mol (median 2.1 %). The training set tops out at ~210 cm³/mol, so extrapolation was checked directly on the measured maltodextrins: error −0.6 % (DP2), −2.0 % (DP3), −1.9 % (DP4), −2.4 % (DP5), −2.6 % (DP6), −2.9 % (DP7, 674 vs 695 cm³/mol), i.e. it underestimates slightly at large size. We therefore quote ±4 % and basis=predicted: udenafil (C25H36N6O4S, est. 399.6 → 399.6 ± 16.0), PMSF (C7H7FO2S, est. 127.2 → 127.2 ± 5.0). Sanity check: 0.77 and 0.73 mL/g, inside the usual 0.70–0.80 mL/g range. Never quote an uncertainty tighter than the validation error of the method that produced the number.
  2. Pick names. iupac_name (lowercased for the JSON key), a common_name, and any aliases people actually write (pmsf, phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride, ...). Check none collides with an existing key first.
  3. Add the TSV row to nonbiological.tsv: commonname, iupacname, formula, charge, v0, uncertainty, basis, doi (a DOI, or a citation/method string when there is none).
  4. Add the JSON entry to nonbiological.json: "<lowercased iupac name>": [electron_count, v0, uncertainty], where electron_count = Σ atomic numbers − charge (udenafil 276, PMSF 90). The key must be entirely lowercase because PMV.jl lowercases every lookup; a mixed-case key can never be found.
  5. Add aliases to common_to_iupac.json (lowercase alias → lowercase iupac key that exists in the JSON). Include a self-mapping for a long IUPAC name.
  6. Check. Every JSON value must equal one of that species' non-rejected TSV rows chosen by the selection rule above, all JSON keys lowercase, every alias target present in the JSON. Then add the solute to the fixture's SOLUTES with a realistic concentration σ, and remove its MISSING FROM PMV LOOKUP note.

Changing a fixture's SOLUTES changes its ρₑ prior, so that fixture's stored res*.txt/.png outputs are stale until it is re-run. All 27 fixtures were re-run after the 2026-10 table merge and buffer audit (see test/fitting_tests/README.md).

IMP disodium salt (2026-10, for SASDUN5's 10 mM IMP buffer): no aqueous V0 for any inosine/guanosine nucleotide was found, so inosine 5'-monophosphate disodium salt (179.9 ± 6.0, basis=predicted) is built with the same additivity the RNA/DNA backends use: inosine 166.1 (the JSON's pick, 10.1021/je500458e) + the phosphate increment AMP·Na − adenosine = 17.7 (Kishore & Ahluwalia 1990, 10.1007/BF00650644; it already contains one Na⁺) + a second Na⁺ (−1.21) + 0.96 × the AMP phosphate pKa2 ionization ΔV (−2.85, Krausz 1972, 10.1021/ja00781a034; 0.96 = dianion fraction at pH 7.5 with pKa2 ≈ 6.1). It inherits the base-independence assumption of the increment and is only valid near neutral pH (dianion). ±6 (≈3 %) is a judgement covering the single-source increment and the 30 °C / 0.1 M KCl conditions of the ΔV, not a validation error.

RNA / DNA

V0(residue) = V0(free nucleoside) + V0(phosphate group increment). The phosphate increment (17.7 cm³/mol) is estimated from the only base with both a measured free-nucleoside and free-nucleotide value in one internally-consistent source (Kishore & Ahluwalia 1990, 10.1007/BF00650644): AMP·Na (189.1) − adenosine (171.4) = 17.7 cm³/mol. (Kishore 1990's own adenosine value is itself a secondhand reproduction of Kishore, Bhat & Ahluwalia, Biophys. Chem. 33 (1989) 227–236. The AMP/adenosine pair is the basis for the whole per-letter table extrapolation for the phosphate-group increments, applied uniformly to G/C/U/T's nucleoside values under a base-independenceassumption.

Ionization

  • ionization.tsv (duplicated in both RNA/ and DNA/ per the phosphate chemistry being shared) has pKa values for adenine/adenosine/guanosine ring protonation-deprotonation equilibria from Christensen, Rytting & Izatt 1970 (10.1021/bi00827a012 .
  • ionization.json is populated using the same single physical anchor.
  • ΔV = 2.85 cm³/mol for all eight letters; Krausz's AMP-only.
  • pKa is per-base and real.
  • adenine/adenosine/guanosine ring ionizations are not covered. (pKa exists, from Christensen/Alberty/Lowe, but no ΔV for any of them.
  • Phosphate pKa2/ΔV: Krausz, Fitzig & Gabbay, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1972, 94(26), 9194–9197 (dilatometry, 30°C, 0.1 M KCl) reports AMP's phosphate-group deprotonation at pKa≈6.0 with ΔV=−2.85 mL/mol.
